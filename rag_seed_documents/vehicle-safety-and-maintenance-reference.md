## Safety controls

- Operate only equipment for which the person is qualified, authorized, and licensed as required by the applicable organization and law.
- Review the manufacturer operator manual and applicable local requirements before operating unfamiliar equipment or using a special mode.
- Do not permit unauthorized operation. Use required restraints and keep controls, exits, and emergency equipment unobstructed.
- Follow applicable traffic and site rules. Drive at a speed that maintains control for the conditions, not merely the posted limit.
- Account for load, surface, weather, visibility, and braking distance. Do not overload equipment or use it outside its intended limits.
- Secure loads, tools, and loose items so they do not obstruct operation or create a hazard.
- Stop and report accidents, damage, unsafe conditions, or equipment defects through the local reporting process.
- Do not operate when alertness is impaired. Take a safe break or transfer operation when drowsy.

## Four-wheel-drive and fire-engine constraints

- Use four-wheel drive only when added traction or power is required and within manufacturer instructions.
- Assess terrain before travel; select the correct gear before entering difficult terrain and respect the vehicle's limits.
- For shifting, hubs, winches, and recovery operations, follow the manufacturer instructions and exact Chapter 2 guidance. Never exceed rated capacity or improvise recovery equipment.
- Only qualified personnel may operate fire-suppression engines. Apply Chapter 3 when this equipment is in scope; do not infer fire-response tactics from this document.

## Preventive maintenance controls

### Before operation

Before use, inspect the equipment for mechanical safety and readiness. Use the source/manual-appropriate inspection for tires, leaks, fluids, lights and signals, visibility equipment, fuel, horn, brakes, emergency equipment, steering, battery, wipers, body condition, and records.

### During and after operation

Remain alert for unusual noise, vibration, instrument readings, leaks, poor braking, improper shifting, steering changes, loose components, or other defects. Take safe corrective action, report deficiencies, and record defects so equipment is ready for its next required use.

### Scheduled service and inspection

- Follow manufacturer service intervals and any stricter local requirements.
- Adjust service frequency for abnormal operating conditions as approved by the responsible supervisor.
- Maintain inspection and service records, including defects, repairs, and deferred work.
- Use qualified mechanics for mechanical inspections and repairs beyond the operator's authorized scope.
- Verify that completed repairs resolved the stated deficiency before returning equipment to service.
